Question 3

How do states and territories compare once population is taken into account?

Raw counts make the biggest states look worst. Per 100,000 people, the picture changes: in 2021 the Northern Territory had 286.1, Queensland 200.3 and New South Wales 111.2.

Hospitalisations per 100,000 people by state and territory

† Data break. Victoria’s counts from 2012 aren’t comparable with 2011, and a definition change in NSW between 2017 and 2018 means later years aren’t comparable with earlier ones.

Source: BITRE hospitalised injury data; population from ABS (state_annual.csv). Map: Natural Earth.

The eight states and territories don’t add up to the national total (33,553 against 34,033 in 2011). That’s expected, because the national figure also includes people with an unrecorded or migratory residence.
